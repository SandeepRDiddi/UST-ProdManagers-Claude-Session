Icebreaker ⏱ 5 min · pairs

60-Second Case Swap

Turn to your neighbour. Each person gets 60 seconds: describe one project where you built exactly what was asked. Your partner guesses — did it get used, or did it quietly die? Then swap.

01Module 1 · 20 min

Product Mindset & Identity Shift

AI makes execution cheap. The bottleneck moves upstream — to whether the ask was even the right one.

  • Old identity: "I execute what's asked." New identity: "I make sure we're solving the right thing, then I execute fast."
  • The recurring failure: dashboards, scores, and tools built to spec that quietly go unused — because no one asked what decision they were meant to change.
  • This isn't "slow down." It's front-load the thinking, then move faster than before on the build.
Pop Quiz · 1

What actually defines the identity shift?

  • A. Type faster with AI autocomplete
  • B. Ask what problem you're really solving, before you build
  • C. Use as many AI tools as possible
  • D. Skip discovery meetings to save time
Answer: B. The shift is judgment, not speed or tool count — AI just removes the excuse for skipping it.
02Module 2 · 20 min

WHY Thinking Before Solutioning

Most prompts are HOW-first. WHY-first inserts a root-cause pass — a Five Whys discipline — before any build.

How-first
"Build me a churn prediction model for our subscription base."
Why-first
"Before we design anything, ask me the questions you'd need answered to be confident we're solving the real driver of churn."
  • The WHY-first version turns Claude from an order-fulfillment engine into a root-cause interviewer.
Pop Quiz · 2

HOW-first or WHY-first?

"Write a script to auto-approve routine prior-authorization requests."

  • A. HOW-first
  • B. WHY-first
Answer: A — HOW-first. It names the build before anyone's asked which denials are procedural vs. clinical judgment. Rewrite it before Demo 2!
Icebreaker ⏱ 5 min · whole room

The Assumption Line-Up

Facilitator reads one line: "Top SKUs keep stocking out at 15 stores, even though total inventory is up 12% this year." Room votes by show of hands — before seeing any data: Forecasting error? Allocation error? Lead-time issue? Need more info? Tally the votes, then open Module 3.

03Module 3 · 15 min

Discovery-Led Framing

Claude's first output should be a problem brief, not a solution. The Discovery Canvas makes that a repeatable habit.

  • 1. Problem statement — what's observably wrong, no solution named.
  • 2. Who's affected & how — the role, the exact moment of pain.
  • 3. Current workaround — what people do today. This is where real requirements hide.
  • 4. Cost of inaction — hours, rework rate, revenue, risk.
  • 5. Definition of success — the observable change that proves it's fixed.
Pop Quiz · 3

Which Discovery Canvas field is this?

"Store managers currently export last week's sales to Excel every Monday and eyeball which SKUs look low."

  • A. Problem statement
  • B. Current workaround
  • C. Definition of success
Answer: B. That's the manual workaround — the richest source of real requirements, and the easiest field to skip.
04Demo 1 · Claude Desktop · 18 min

The Stockout Paradox

Total inventory is up 12% year-over-year. Top SKUs still stock out at 15 stores every peak weekend. Real, common retail pattern.

Case: Retail Ops

Real dataset · 10,800 rows · synthetic, no client data
  1. Anti-pattern shown first: "Build me a reorder dashboard" — Claude complies instantly, no discovery.
  2. Reframe: "Ask me your top questions before you design anything."
  3. Claude separates the hypotheses: forecasting error vs. allocation error vs. lead-time variance.
  4. Claude runs the numbers live against the store-level CSV using code execution: correlation(lead time, stockouts) = 0.98 · correlation(demand variance, stockouts) = 0.07.
  5. Root cause narrows to one variable — not the one the room voted for.
Verdict West-region stores (long lead time) stock out on 45.5% of store-days vs. 14.5% for the rest of the network. A generic dashboard would have missed this entirely.
Pop Quiz · 4

What was the real root cause?

  • A. Not enough total inventory
  • B. Long replenishment lead time at specific stores
  • C. Wrong SKUs stocked entirely
  • D. Website checkout errors
Answer: B. Total inventory was never the problem — allocation and lead time were.
05Demo 2 · Claude Code CLI · 11 min

The Prior-Authorization Backlog

A large share of denials trace back to missing documentation — not genuine clinical judgment calls. Both sit in the same manual queue.

Case: Payer Ops

Real dataset · 70 requests · synthetic, no patient data
  1. Anti-pattern shown first: "Auto-approve requests matching policy keywords" — unsafe, conflates two very different case types.
  2. Reframe: Claude Code reads the sample requests + policy doc and splits procedural gaps from clinical judgment calls.
  3. Split found: 59.3% of denials are procedural (missing documentation), 40.7% are genuine clinical judgment calls.
  4. Automation is scoped deliberately: only procedural / missing-info cases get flagged for fast human fix-up.
  5. Clinical-judgment cases route to a human reviewer — a human-in-the-loop design, not full automation.
Verdict Of 70 requests: 16 approved, 32 flagged procedural, 22 routed to clinical review. Safe automation starts with the boring, procedural bucket — not the hard judgment calls.
Pop Quiz · 5

Which prior-auth cases get automated first?

  • A. All of them
  • B. Only the hard clinical-judgment cases
  • C. Only procedural / missing-information cases
  • D. None — automation is never safe here
Answer: C. Scope automation to what's safe and defensible — leave judgment calls to people.
