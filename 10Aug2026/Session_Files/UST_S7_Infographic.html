01Concept · 15 min

Engineering the Client Narrative

CIOs don't buy architecture. They buy a resolved tension. SCQA — Situation, Complication, Question, Answer — is the structure; the discipline is translating every technical fact into the vocabulary a CIO already thinks in: TCO, risk-adjusted return, board reporting, integration exposure.

Situation
Accounts Payable processes ~1,800 invoices/month, fully manual, at roughly $25,200/month in processing cost.
Complication
Invoice volume grows with the business; headcount doesn't scale the same way, and manual review fatigue is where duplicate-payment and fraud exposure actually lives — the risk already exists today, it's just unmeasured.
Question
How do we cut processing cost materially without increasing the very risk manual review is supposed to control?
Answer
Tiered autonomy: full automation only where risk is provably low and testable, hard governance and human review everywhere else — not a binary "turn AI loose on payments" decision.

Translation discipline: "agentic workflow" becomes "automated only where three specific conditions are met." "Guardrails" becomes "hard-coded thresholds a code reviewer, not a runtime setting, controls." Say the CIO-native version first; the technical version is available if asked.

Pop Quiz · 1

In SCQA, what comes immediately before the Answer?

  • A. Situation
  • B. Complication
  • C. Question
  • D. Objection
Answer: C. The Question is what earns the right to the Answer — skip it, and a recommendation just sounds like an opinion.
02Concept · 15 min

The Autonomy Objection — Levels of Autonomy

Real framework: Sheridan & Verplank's Levels of Automation, the same lineage behind the SAE self-driving levels most CIOs already recognize as an analogy. Autonomy is a dial, not a switch — and the dial can be set differently per risk category.

Tier 4
Full autonomy. Exact PO match, established vendor (6mo+), ≤ $10,000. No human touch.
Tier 3
Conditional autonomy. PO matched within 5% variance, established vendor, ≤ $50,000. Auto-approved, logged, 10% sampled for human audit.
Tier 2
Assisted. New vendor, larger mismatch, or $50k–$250k. Agent recommends; a human approves every one.
Tier 1
Manual only. No PO, duplicate-suspect, or over $250,000. Full manual handling, no exceptions.

The objection: "We're not comfortable letting AI approve payments on its own." The reframe: nobody's proposing Tier 4 for everything — it's scoped to the lowest-risk 61.6% of volume, with the boundary enforced in code, not policy alone.

Pop Quiz · 2

Which tiers require a human to approve every single transaction?

  • A. Tier 4 only
  • B. Tier 3 only
  • C. Tier 2 and Tier 1
  • D. None — all tiers are autonomous
Answer: C. Tier 2 gets an agent recommendation but always needs a human decision; Tier 1 doesn't even get a recommendation — full manual, by design.
03Concept · 15 min

The Governance Objection — NIST AI RMF

Real framework: the U.S. NIST AI Risk Management Framework — four functions, Govern / Map / Measure / Manage. Each one shows up as a concrete design decision in the AP agent, not an abstract compliance gesture.

Govern
Tier thresholds are hard-coded and versioned in the agent's source — changeable only through code review, never a runtime parameter that can silently drift.
Map
Every autonomous decision records which specific rule fired — never a bare "approved" with no reasoning attached.
Measure
Tier distribution and dollar exposure are computed and printed on every single run — not buried in a log nobody opens.
Manage
Every Tier 1/2 invoice routes to a human queue, full stop; every Tier 3 decision has a live sampling rate for human audit.

The objection: "How do we know this stays in compliance?" The reframe: don't assert it — five governance guarantees are written as automated tests and run against the real data, live, in the demo.

Pop Quiz · 3

Which NIST AI RMF function is served by printing tier distribution and dollar exposure on every run?

  • A. Govern
  • B. Map
  • C. Measure
  • D. Manage
Answer: C. Measure is about visible, ongoing tracking — exactly what a printed summary on every run provides.
04Concept · 15 min

The Cost Objection — TCO & Risk-Adjusted ROI

Total Cost of Ownership, not just a subscription line: build cost, governance overhead, and operational cost, set against a real manual baseline.

Build
$38,000 one-time (400 engineering hours at a $95/hr blended rate)
Governance
$2,400/month (20 governance hours + audit tooling)
Operational
$5,586/month, all tiers combined, vs. $25,200/month manual baseline
Net benefit
$17,214/month net · 2.2-month payback · $168,568 Year-1 net · $581,704 3-Year net

The objection: "This sounds too good to be true." The reframe: state the catch honestly — 11.9% of volume (Tier 1) sees zero automation benefit and stays fully manual, by design. The ROI is credible precisely because it isn't claiming to automate everything.

Pop Quiz · 4

What's the payback period on the $38,000 build cost?

  • A. 2.2 months
  • B. 12 months
  • C. 24 months
  • D. It never pays back
Answer: A. $38,000 build ÷ $17,214 net monthly benefit ≈ 2.2 months.

05Script · 5 min

Objection-Handling Script — Acknowledge → Reframe → Evidence

Autonomy
Acknowledge: "That's the right instinct to have with payments." Reframe: "This isn't all-or-nothing — it's scoped to the lowest-risk 61.6% of volume." Evidence: the 4-tier rule table and the hard dollar thresholds enforced in code.
Governance
Acknowledge: "Compliance has to sign off on this, not just IT." Reframe: "We don't ask you to trust it — we show you the test suite." Evidence: 5/5 governance guarantees, run live, against real data.
Cost
Acknowledge: "A number this good deserves scrutiny." Reframe: "The catch is stated up front: 11.9% of volume gets zero automation, on purpose." Evidence: the TCO table and the 2.2-month payback, computed, not projected.
Pop Quiz · 5

In Acknowledge → Reframe → Evidence, what comes last, and why?

  • A. Acknowledge — always end by validating the concern
  • B. Reframe — always end by repositioning the question
  • C. Evidence — because a reframed claim without proof is still just an assertion
  • D. None — order doesn't matter
Answer: C. Acknowledge earns the room's trust, Reframe repositions the concern, but only Evidence actually closes it — which is why every objection above ends with something tested or computed, not asserted.
