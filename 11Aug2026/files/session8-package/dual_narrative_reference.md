# Dual Narrative — Panel vs. UST
(Reference example for Lab 3 — Articulate Value to the Panel AND to UST)

Same evidence, two audiences, two different Complications and two different
stakes. Both are true at once; neither is the "real" one.

---

## To the Panel (playing the client / CIO)

**Why this matters to you:** because the $19,614 a month you'd save is the
least interesting number in this pitch. The interesting number is 5 of 5 —
every governance guarantee we're claiming is a test that runs against real
data, not a policy document you have to take on faith. We're not asking you
to trust that this is safe. We're showing you the test suite and inviting
you to try to break it. The tiering means you're never choosing between
"fast" and "controlled" — the 11.9% of volume that carries real risk gets
exactly the same manual scrutiny it gets today, untouched. You're not
buying a black box. You're buying a system that tells you, on every single
run, exactly what it did and why.

## To UST (internal)

**Why this matters to us:** because this isn't a one-off AP integration —
it's a reusable pattern. Tiered autonomy plus a tested governance layer is
the same shape whether the domain is Accounts Payable, claims adjudication,
or prior-authorization triage. The hard part of every one of those
conversations is the same hard part: proving autonomy is safe before a risk
or compliance team will sign off on it. We now have a demonstrated,
governance-tested reference build we can point to in the room, not a
hypothetical architecture on a slide. That's a sales asset as much as a
delivery asset — it shortens the trust-building phase of every future
autonomous-agent engagement, in any domain that shares this risk shape.
It's also a capability signal: a team that ships tested guardrails, not
just working code, is the team a risk-averse enterprise buyer picks over a
competitor who can't show the same rigor.

## The One Thread Connecting Both

Both narratives point at the same evidence — the tier rules, the governance
tests, the ROI numbers — because a pitch that needs different *facts* for
different audiences isn't a narrative, it's two separate claims hoping
nobody compares notes. What changes between the panel and UST is which
consequence gets emphasized, never the underlying truth.
